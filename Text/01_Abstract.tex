\section*{\textit {Abstract}} %%%% (DO NOT CHANGE) %%%%

This study addresses a fundamental question: can sample observations from the same class and different areas be modeled by the same distribution?
Motivated by the increasing anthropic pressures over the Cerrado, and the rise of SAR data for change detection, we gather statistical evidence about the spatial behavior of sample distributions of the Cerrado's forest, savanna, and grassland formations from NISAR and Sentinel-1 SAR imagery.
We compared the goodness of fit of $\Gamma_{SAR}$ and $\mathcal{G}^0_I$ models, and assessing pairwise similarity using the Kullback--Leibler hypothesis test.
The $\mathcal{G}^0_I$ model best described all classes, polarizations, and missions.
However, pairwise tests revealed substantial within-class differences tied to mean brightness, indicating that a single parameterization is inadequate to describe the sample observations in the spatial domain.
Likelihood-ratio tests supported sharing the roughness parameter $\alpha$ while allowing $\mu$ to 
vary, except for NISAR Grassland (HH/HV).
These findings suggest representing classes as families of $\mathcal{G}^0_I$ distributions.
