This study evaluates the spatial-domain distributions of Cerrado's Forest, Savanna, and Grassland formations samples from NISAR and Sentinel-1 SAR imagery.
We compare the goodness of fit of the $\Gamma_{SAR}$ and $\mathcal G^0_I$ models and assess pairwise sample similarity using the Kullback--Leibler hypothesis test.
The $\mathcal G^0_I$ model was found to be the better model to describe the sample distributions of all classes, polarizations and missions.
Pairwise tests nevertheless revealed substantial within-class differences, primarily associated with mean brightness, indicating that a single parameterization may not adequately represent all samples in a class.
Likelihood-ratio tests generally did not reject a common roughness parameter across samples, supporting models that share $\alpha$ while allowing $\mu$ to vary.
The exception was NISAR Grassland, for which the common $\alpha$ hypothesis was rejected in both HH and HV polarizations.
These findings motivate representing classes as families of $\mathcal G^0_I$ distributions and evaluating this approach with broader datasets and classification tasks.
