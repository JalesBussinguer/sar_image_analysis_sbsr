\section{Results and Discussion}

We tested the goodness-of-fit of all \qty{5}{} samples of each class for the $\Gamma_{\text{SAR}}$ and the $\mathcal G^0_I$ models.
Figure~\ref{fig:gof-rejection} presents the percentage of null hypothesis rejection on the tested models, according to its mission, class and band.

\begin{figure}[h]
    \begin{center}
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/gof_rejection_rates.pdf}
    \caption{Rejection of the goodness-of-fit null hypothesis under $\bm\Gamma_{\text{SAR}}$ and $\bm{\mathcal G}^{\bm 0}_{\bm I}$ ($\bm\alpha=\qty{1}{\percent}$; \qty{5}{} samples per class and polarization).}
    \label{fig:gof-rejection}
    \end{center}
\end{figure}

For NISAR, the majority of the samples show a clear adherence to the $\mathcal G^0_I$ distribution, since only two out of \qty{30} samples rejected the fit.
By contrast, almost all classes rejected the fit for $\Gamma_{\text{SAR}}$, except the Forest Class in the $\text{HH}$ polarization.
This result indicates that only samples of this class and polarization are likely to present a full developed speckle.
The $\mathcal G^0_I$ was also found to be the best model to characterize the Sentinel-1 sample distributions, since it presented less rejections to the fit than $\Gamma_{\text{SAR}}$.
We also considered the tractability and interpretation of the parameters of $\mathcal G^0_I$ in its adoption.

Under the $\mathcal G^0_I$ distribution, we tested the similarity/separability of the classes' samples pairwise using the $S_{\mathrm{KL}}$ hypothesis test.
For the sake of conciseness, we only report the rate of H$_{0}$ rejection per class and polarization.
The results of the tests are presented in Table~\ref{tab:kl-rejection}.

\vspace{0.3cm}

For NISAR, all classes and polarizations had similarity rejections, but in different proportions.
For the Forest class, both polarizations presented the lowest rates of H$_{0}$ rejection, indicating the highest similarity among all classes.
This result is directly related to the goodness-of-fit result that points to a fully developed speckle characteristic of the samples.
This result indicates that this class and polarization can be characterized by a single model.
Further investigation is encouraged to test this hypothesis.
The Savanna and Grassland classes presented higher rates of H$_{0}$ rejection, and, thus, the least similarity.
The results for Sentinel-1 present an almost uniform H$_{0}$ rejection at high rates.
All classes in all polarizations presented more than \qty{60}{\percent} of similarity rejection.

The results for NISAR and Sentinel-1 indicate that there is no complete similarity between the classes' samples in the spatial domain in neither polarizations.
This leads to a scenario where a single parameterization of the model can not explain the samples distributions in the spatial domain.

Since the $\mathcal G^0_I$ model is parameterized by the mean brightness ($\mu$) and shape ($\alpha$) parameters ($L$ is fixed as in Ref.~\cite{alpala2025}), we tried to qualify the H$_{0}$ rejections in the $S_{\mathrm{KL}}$ tests.
We used the scenarios described in Ref.~\cite{nascimento2010} to understand the cause of H$_{0}$ rejections; cf.\ Table~\ref{tab:kl-causes}.

\vspace{0.3cm}

The main cause of H$_{0}$ rejection for both missions was the difference in the mean brightness between the pairs of samples.
The results show that \qtyrange[range-phrase = \text{--}]{76}{95.24}{\percent} of H$_{0}$ rejections among all classes and polarizations fall within this scenario.
At this point, in perspective with the results from $S_{\mathrm{KL}}$, this result points to the fact that a single parameterization of $\mathcal G^0_I$ is not sufficient to describe an entire class in the spatial domain.
Instead of a single distribution with fixed parameters $\alpha$ and $\mu$, the sample distributions might be characterized by multiple parameterizations of the model where the mean brightness is their key difference and the shape parameter is shared.
We call these multiple parameterizations for the same class a family of distributions.

To verify the hypothesis of multiple parameterizations, we executed another hypothesis test of equality between models with common $\alpha$.
The results are shown in Table~\ref{tab:common-alpha}.

\vspace{0.3cm}

These results indicate that in general, it is possible to characterize an entire class with a common shape parameter.
This result is especially valuable, since the roughness parameter is closely related to the number of elementary scatterers in each pixel \cite{yue2021}.
Given the missions' frequency, the $\alpha$ parameter is coherent with the expected number of elementary scatterers for each class.

The only exception was the NISAR Grassland class in both $\text{HH}$ and $\text{HV}$ polarizations.
For this specific class, the results indicate that not a single model is capable of explaining the class's spatial distributions.
Further investigations are encouraged to verify which models best describe this class.
