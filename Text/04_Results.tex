\section{Results and Discussion}

We tested the goodness-of-fit of all \qty{5}{} samples of each class for the $\Gamma_{\text{SAR}}$ and the $\mathcal G^0_I$ models.
Figure \ref{fig:gof-rejection} presents the percentage of null hypothesis rejection on the tested models, according to its mission, class and band.

\begin{figure}[h]
    \begin{center}
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/rejection_rates.png}
    \caption{Rejection of the goodness-of-fit null hypothesis under $\bm\Gamma_{\text{SAR}}$ and $\bm{\mathcal G}^{\bm 0}_{\bm I}$ ($\bm\alpha=\qty{1}{\percent}$; \qty{5}{} samples per class and polarization).}
    \label{fig:gof-rejection}
    \end{center}
\end{figure}


For NISAR, the majority of the samples show a clear fit to the $\mathcal G^0_I$ distribution, since the rejection of fit is restricted to two samples of \qty{30}{} in total.
On the other hand, almost all classes rejected the fit for $\Gamma_{\text{SAR}}$, except the Forest Class in the HH polarization.
This result indicates that the samples of this specific class and polarization are likely to present a full developed speckle.
The $\mathcal G^0_I$ was also found to be the best model to characterize the Sentinel-1 sample distributions, since it presented less rejections to the fit than $\Gamma_{\text{SAR}}$.
We also considered the tractability and interpretation of the parameters of $\mathcal G^0_I$ in its adoption.

Under the $\mathcal G^0_I$ distribution, we tested the similarity/separability of the classes' samples pairwise using the $S_{\mathrm{KL}}$ hypothesis test.
For the sake of conciseness, we only report the rate of H$_{0}$ rejection per class and polarization.
The results of the tests are presented in Table \ref{tab:kl-rejection}.

\vspace{0.3cm}

For NISAR, all classes and polarizations had similarity rejections, but in different proportions.
For the Forest class, both polarizations presented the lowest rates of H$_{0}$ rejection, indicating the highest similarity among all classes.
This result is directly related to the goodness-of-fit result that points to a fully developed speckle characteristic of the samples.
At this point, this result indicates that this specific class and polarization can be characterized by a single model in the spatial domain.
Further investigation is encouraged to test this hypothesis.
The Savanna and Grassland classes presented higher rates of H$_{0}$ rejection, and thus the least similarity.
The results for Sentinel-1 present an almost uniform H$_{0}$ rejection at high rates.
All classes in all polarizations presented more than 60$\%$ of similarity rejection.

The results for NISAR and Sentinel-1 indicates that there is no complete similarity between the classes' samples in the spatial domain in neither polarizations.
This leads to a scenario where a single parameterization of the model can not explain the samples distributions in the spatial domain.

Since the $\mathcal G^0_I$ is parameterized in terms of the mean brightness ($\mu$) and roughness ($\alpha$) parameters ($L$ is fixed) \cite{alpala2025}, we tried to qualify the H$_{0}$ rejections in the $S_{\mathrm{KL}}$ tests.
We used the scenarios described in \cite{nascimento2010} to understand the cause of H$_{0}$ rejections (Table \ref{tab:kl-causes}).

\vspace{0.3cm}

The main cause of H$_{0}$ rejection for both missions was the difference in the mean brightness between the pairs of sample distributions.
The results show that \qtyrange[range-phrase = \text{--}]{76}{95.24}{\percent} of H$_{0}$ rejection among all classes and polarizations fall within this scenario.
At this point, in perspective with the results from $S_{\mathrm{KL}}$, this result points that a single parameterization of $\mathcal G^0_I$ is not sufficient to describe an entire class in the spatial domain.
Instead of a single and monolithic distribution with fixed parameters $\alpha$ and $\mu$, the sample distributions might be characterized by multiple parameterizations of the model where the mean brightness is their key difference and the roughness parameter is shared.
We call these multiple parameterizations for the same class as a family of distributions.

To verify the hypothesis of multiple parameterizations that characterizes the samples distributions of the same class, we executed another hypothesis test of equality between the distributions with a common $\alpha$.
The results are shown in Table \ref{tab:common-alpha}.

\vspace{0.3cm}

These results indicate that in general, it is possible to characterize an entire class by calculating a common roughness that will be shared for all sample distributions.
This result is especially valuable, since the roughness parameter is closely related to the number of elementary backscatterers in each pixel \cite{gambini2015}.
Given the missions' frequency, the $\alpha$ parameter is coherent with the expected number of elementary backscatterers for each class.

The only exception was the NISAR Grassland class in both $\text{HH}$ and $\text{HV}$ polarizations.
For this specific class, the results indicates that not a single model is capable to explain the class spatial distributions.
Further investigations are encouraged to verify which models best describe this class in NISAR data.
