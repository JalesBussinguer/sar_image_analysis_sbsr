\section{Conclusions}

The $\mathcal G^0_I$ distribution provided the best fit to the data for both missions.
However, the Kullback--Leibler tests revealed differences among samples, mainly associated with mean brightness, indicating that a single point in the parameter space does not fully describe each class in the spatial domain.
For most classes and polarizations, the hypothesis of a common shape parameter was not rejected, suggesting that distributions may share $\alpha$ while differing in $\mu$.
The exception was the NISAR Grassland class, for which this hypothesis was rejected in both HH and HV.
These results support characterizing classes using families of $\mathcal G^0_I$ distributions rather than a single model with fixed parameters.

The findings of this study can contribute to SAR-based strategies to monitor the Cerrado native vegetation that complements strategies such as DETER-Cerrado.
It points towards a direction where the strategies must be designed at a local level to detect disturbances over the native vegetation, especially using the shape parameter of the distributions as a proxy of the vegetation condition over time.

Future work should investigate which models best characterize the Grassland class in NISAR data; how seasonality impacts the sample distributions over time, and whether $\mathcal G^0_I$ families improve land-cover classification.
